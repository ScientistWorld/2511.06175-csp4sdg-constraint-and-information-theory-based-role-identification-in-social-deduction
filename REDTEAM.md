# Does this gym reward better research?

The tested honest improvements and ablations score in the right order, above the observed seed spread. **The gym nevertheless fails the broader test:** it rewards invalid constant outputs, exposed answers, changed labels, and missing results. A scientist-created method whitelist can also suppress a genuinely improved method submitted under a new name. The paper's headline CSP-versus-LLM comparison remains unverified because the reproduction contains no evaluated LLM baseline. The reduced benchmark retains a small hypotheses-versus-Strict effect, but does not establish the paper's broader claims about assertions, dialogue, or calibrated inference.

All numbers below come from the unchanged, no-argument `bash scripts/evaluate_test.sh`. There are 26 saved attempts, including 15 shortcut checks. Each has its own `redteam/<name>/run.sh`, installation log, test log, and `scores_test.json`. The scientist receives no initial `scoring/scores.json`; each run explicitly constructs its comparison whitelist from visible player counts and Strict/+HypIG setting names. No reference score values are used to construct an attempt. [summary.tsv](redteam/summary.tsv) and [summary.json](redteam/summary.json) contain the complete metric vectors, deltas, split counts, and calculations.

**Score conventions.** The default test writes six AvalonLogs experiments, each with six marginal metrics. For readability, `Q` is their experiment-weighted mean, with the active experiment weights renormalized; it is a diagnostic, **not the ranking formula**. `R` uses the specified ranking formula against the `CSP_HypIG` values in `scoring/reference.json`: sum original, unrenormalized experiment weight times absolute coefficient times improvement in the better direction, divided by that metric's population standard deviation across these 26 audit attempts. All 36 active coefficients are 1 and all directions are higher-is-better. The cached reproduced method has `Q=0.502209, R=-0.031687`; the paper comparator has `Q=0.521453, R=0`. Numeric ranks describe this audit cohort; future attempts and their standard deviations are unknown. Complete-vector dominance and exact ties do not depend on those standard deviations. Missing metrics are skipped, exactly as specified.

**Honest variants.** The private-knowledge fix uses Merlin's complete permitted list of visible evil players: a player absent from that list cannot have a Merlin-visible evil role. The original inference enforces positive membership only. The fixed view uses the original raw-predicate IG rule consistently; the other views retain their cached predictions. Thus this is a real inference fix using permitted information, with a small IG-version change in the modified view for the mixed-version games described below.

**Seed spread and reproduction.** [Seeds 0](redteam/reference_seed_0/scores_test.json), [1](redteam/reference_seed_1/scores_test.json), and [2](redteam/reference_seed_2/scores_test.json) rebuild the current visible conditional-hypothesis IG inference on the same 12,091 games. Each gives `Q=0.5029678604, R=-0.0304503722`; the maximum range of any test metric is **0.0000**, and the Q and R ranges are zero. Inference is exhaustive and deterministic; these seeds do not regenerate the converted data. They measure run stability, not corpus sampling uncertainty or generalization. The three auxiliary `raw_ig_seed_*` reruns are also identical (`Q=0.5020973048`). The honest gains and losses exceed the observed score spread.

The visible solver and supplied predictions do not describe one reproducible implementation. [source_probe.json](redteam/source_probe.json) identifies 414 raw-IG-only and 427 conditional-IG-only 9-player files, plus one agreeing with both and one with neither; 10-player files split 166 raw-only and 131 conditional-only. The unexplained 9-player file is `2020-04-04T02:53:39_constraints.json`. Raw IG matches all rounded 5–8-player test metrics. Rebuilding current source everywhere changes individual posterior entries by as much as 0.3 and raises Q by 0.0007590641. This is a provenance discrepancy, not seed noise. The exact grouped inference used to make full reruns affordable agrees with direct current-source inference to 1.34e-15 in the checked 5/6-player games, and with literal 10-player enumeration to 2.97e-14.

Identified departures and limitations relative to [the local paper](paper/2511.06175.md):

- The paper evaluates Avalon-NLU, Mafia-UCB, and AvalonLogs, including truthful/deceptive conditions and LLM/hybrid comparisons. The no-argument test covers only AvalonLogs. The supplied comparison whitelist selects two cached CSP settings; without it, three extra settings appear for 5/9-player games only. Assertions, manual weights, turn variants, dialogue reasoning, and hybrid improvements are outside this test.
- The corpus is real public data, not synthetic. The paper reports 12,699 games; the workspace has 12,402 raw logs, 12,394 converted games, and 12,091 solved artifacts. In particular, only 297 of 600 converted 10-player games have predictions. The hidden slice contains 6,043 games: 1,679/1,588/1,367/840/421/148 for sizes 5/6/7/8/9/10. Coverage follows available outputs rather than a fixed independent manifest.
- The paper samples one quest per AvalonLogs game. This gym averages all available completed quest states, then games, changing the amount and temporal distribution of evidence scored.
- Converted solved games contain 21,172 evidence items, 639,654 hypotheses, **zero assertions and zero phenomena**. The paper's concluding event-assertion mechanism is therefore untested. A limited hypothesis-weighting effect survives; a better method on this reduced task is not established to improve the full paper problem. Appendix F explicitly says AvalonLogs constraints are extracted programmatically, so lack of an LLM extractor for this dataset is not itself a departure.
- The local converter uses a global RNG seeded 42 and excludes the rejecting voter when sampling a suspect; the bundled official converter uses seed 0 and can select the voter. Converter `--seed` controls file sampling rather than that global conversion RNG. Fixed converted constraints are reused in the audit.
- Private-role knowledge is incompletely enforced in the reproduced solver, as the honest fix demonstrates. Cached files mix two IG definitions. The alternate `avalon_logs_solver_fast.py` additionally constructs perspective extras without applying them (code inspection); it was not used as the reference implementation in this audit.
- The briefing requires calibrated distributions and MAP assignments. Active ranking scores neither calibration with a proper scoring rule nor MAP. Expected true-role mass is linear in the submitted distribution, so it encourages confident guesses rather than truthful uncertainty; even valid probabilities alone would not resolve that scientific mismatch.

There is no trained reference checkpoint in this training-free method. The artifacts treated as fixed are the cached predictions, role labels, game pool, and method whitelist; replacements of each are exercised above. The label and probability shortcuts also overwrite the embedded Strict comparator, although their success does not depend on moving that baseline. Direct score-file writing is the tested failure case. Timings and success flags do not contribute to the active score.

**Why these results pay.** The default test reads saved predictions without executing inference. It obtains ground truth and game size from their mutable `players` field. It neither checks probability normalization/bounds nor validates declared role composition or MAP. It selects a deterministic hash-sorted half of whatever output files exist; the visible training evaluator exposes the split construction. Thus even an unseen test evaluator does not supply unseen game answers or a fixed test pool. Removing difficult games, views, or methods changes what is measured.

Missing-output attempts emit empty result objects, not zeros for every expected metric and experiment. Under the specified rank, zero terms are added and R is zero; failure can tie the paper comparator and avoid the reproduced method's negative R. Further, when a writable whitelist exists, the hidden test **does not write every method present on disk** into `results`: the improved `FixedKnowledge` setting is completely absent. A method outside that whitelist must be added or installed under an existing whitelisted setting to receive its real metrics. The scientist initially gets no `scoring/scores.json`, and without that file filtering is inactive. The actual [visibility comparison](redteam/reference/whitelist_visibility_check.json) confirms identical game pools and all 72 Strict/HypIG metric cells with and without it. Removing the filter additionally exposes Assert, HypM, and TurnIG settings at 5 and 9 players, so the complete files differ. Every attempt now constructs its own whitelist from visible game sizes and setting names; the completed [visibility replay](redteam/visibility_replay.json) regenerated every attempt and reproduced all 26 earlier score files exactly.

The combined score has no objective-quality gate. The failed-objective shortcut has six losses and thirty gains and receives R=+0.510587 under the actual audit-cohort normalization. This is a demonstrated compensation failure, not a claim that tradeoff ordering is independent of all possible future standard deviations. Coefficient sign cannot prevent it because only magnitude counts; changing units cannot fix it because improvements are standardized per metric.

**Brute force check.** The largest observed assignment spaces by size are 120, 360, 2,520, 6,720, 15,120, and 151,200 worlds. Literal enumeration of a maximal 10-player example took 0.363 seconds including input search; complete scoring/validation took 12.172 seconds, leaving 106,560 feasible worlds after the final public quest. Its objective true-role mass was only 0.218918. The final full-dataset brute-force inference run took 136.118 seconds, and passed the hidden test. Other complete rebuilds took longer with cold filesystem caches and a worker migration; no efficiency metric is scored. **Brute force is feasible, but it does not solve hidden roles perfectly.** The paper itself explicitly uses exhaustive enumeration at these sizes and leaves MCMC for larger games. This is therefore an honest alternative, not evidence that enumeration defeats the paper's intended contribution. This gym cannot rank scalable inference above an exhaustive equivalent: it scores no time or larger-lobby behavior.

The resumed [second job](redteam/job_logs/4cc59711-92b/stdout.log) completed with exit 0 and no computation errors. Checkpointed results were reused. That assertion was corrected to compare the two ranked methods and separately record the extra settings. The [final CPU job](redteam/job_logs/3e7558f4-e64/stdout.log) completed with exit 0 after checkpoint recovery from another worker drain. It regenerated all 26 attempts using explicitly constructed visible whitelists and reproduced every earlier score file exactly; the complete replay took 47.8 minutes including migration and recovery. All temporary method, scoring, output, and runner changes were restored with git against the initial revision recorded in [initial_head.txt](redteam/initial_head.txt); restoration checks are saved in [restoration_check.json](redteam/restoration_check.json). The initial container-owned README/manifest device substitutions, missing trajectory files, and untracked git shim were preserved. Audit artifacts live only in `redteam/` and this report, apart from the required job-control `action.yaml`.

For the next gym iteration, fix the input/game manifest and labels independently of predictions, run submitted inference on permitted views, require complete finite normalized outputs for every expected cell, assign failures their worst valid values, and discover submitted methods independently of writable comparison scores. Restore the missing paper comparisons and assertion-bearing settings before claiming the broader scientific effect. A proper probability score and an objective-performance requirement would align ranking more closely with the stated task.
